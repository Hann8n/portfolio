Gym Tracker

Creator · Oct 2024 to present

Virginia Tech students check how busy the gym is before they walk over. The university's app showed that number, but students rated it poorly. I built a faster alternative, put the crowd count on the home screen, and grew it to more than 4,000 installs and 450 daily active users without spending anything on ads. It holds a 5.0 rating on the App Store from 14 reviews.

How it grew

Students who searched the App Store for the gym found the official app first, with its low rating, and Gym Tracker right next to it with five stars. I wrote the listing to win that comparison: what it does in one line, screenshots that show the crowd count straight away, and a fast path to a first review.

The rest was word of mouth. The app answers one question in a second, so people showed it to the friends they lift with. It had 216 downloads on launch day, and every semester since has started bigger than the last: 604 downloads in August 2025, 928 in August 2026.

What I focused on

  • One number, fast. Widgets put the crowd level on the home screen, so most people never need to open the app.
  • Reasons to come back. I added Rec Sports events and a campus ID pass, so the app stays useful at the door, not just before the walk over.
  • Earning reviews. A 5.0 rating next to a poorly rated official app was the main acquisition channel, so I protected it.

Selling ads to local businesses

With a student audience in place, I built a sponsorship system to sell placements directly to Blacksburg businesses. Sponsored cards sit between the gym counts and the events list. Behind them is an admin tool for setting up creative, scheduling flight dates, and switching ads on and off.

Each campaign produces a report a sponsor can read without help: ad views, unique reach, frequency, link taps and tap rate, with a short note on how each one is measured.

What I took from it

The product did the marketing because it solved an everyday problem better than the official option, and the App Store put the two side by side. When I market something now, I start by asking where people compare it to what they already use.
